# Research notes — competitive voice (2026-07-13)

Condensed for Claude Design. Full narrative lived in the brand conversation; this is the durable extract.

---

## Category winners’ language patterns

### OpenSnow
- Headline energy: *Weather Forecast + Snow Report* — functional.
- Trust: “most accurate,” “trusted source,” “plan their missions.”
- Users praise **no overhype** vs sites that get “overexuberant.”
- Almost **zero ski-bro slang** on marketing surfaces.
- Implication for PeakCam: credibility beats vibe.

### Snow-Forecast
- Signature UI copy pattern: **“where’s the snow in the next 3 days?”**
- Question-as-navigation works; data-first.
- Implication: **Where’s it good?** sits in a proven pattern family.

### Snoflo
- “Climate intelligence for the great outdoors.”
- Section questions: “What the snow is doing,” “Find the freshest snow.”
- Plain English, zero cosplay.
- Implication: plain questions > clever slogans.

### OnTheSnow
- “Worldwide leader…” feature laundry lists.
- Weak personality; PeakCam should not copy corporate SEO voice either.

### Arapahoe Basin — “No B.S. Just Skiing” (2025 campaign)
- Industry trend: anti-frills, mountain as star.
- PeakCam can share the *anti-hype* spirit but should stay a **tool**, not a resort manifesto.

### “Get the goods”
- Used by major resort social (e.g. Whistler `#GetTheGoods`) and tourism/guide copy.
- PeakCam’s “Got the goods” reads as **derivative resort marketing**, not insider product voice.

## What skiers want from multi-resort tools (synthesis)

- Know what to expect for the day  
- Where to go on a storm cycle  
- Accuracy without hype  
- Cams as ground truth when reports disagree  
- One place instead of five resort tabs

Not: coolest powder culture brand.  
Yes: **fastest honest glance** — cams + numbers, free, dual hemisphere.

Visual can stay romantic (poster).
