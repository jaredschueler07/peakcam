**Research date: September 9, 2026 | Owner: Jared Schueler**

This playbook contains the First 25 account briefs and personalized drafts, seven reusable sequences, eight sales materials, starter deal structures and a solo-founder sales routine. The separate CRM contains 100 organizations and a ranked First 20 resort cohort.

Remove the two placeholder camera entries before sending product pitches.

## How to use this on the next workday
Repair the placeholder listings, select Loveland, Monarch, Ski Cooper, Sunlight and Granby from First 25, re-open their public contact pages, and send a brief permission/feedback request. These are relationship-opening messages, not promises of valuable ad reach. In parallel, ask Roundshot about a small sharing-partner pilot. After each response, record the exact person authorized to approve the relevant scope; a marketing reply is not automatically a copyright license.

All dollar values and response probabilities are **planning proposals**, not offers made by these organizations. A vendor may charge PeakCam; a creator may generate traffic rather than pay; an affiliate program may reject the application.

## Pricing logic
Use a buyer’s measurable value, not national traffic alone. Compute destination-specific effective CPM and cost per qualified click/lead, and show them honestly. A $100 pilot on 500 impressions is a $200 effective CPM: that may be unjustifiable unless there is a valuable lead or service component. Do not hide this by quoting whole-site pageviews. Low traffic can justify a free accuracy/distribution experiment, not an inflated ad price.

When raising prices, specify added inventory, verified local audience, outcomes or service work. Avoid unlimited creative changes and broad exclusivity. A provider license should have an affordable minimum, clear cap and termination path; do not promise a percentage of all company revenue for a few cameras.

## Simple order-form checklist — counsel review before use
Legal parties and authorized contacts; exact page(s)/camera(s); permitted assets and rights; start/end dates; price, tax, payment method and collection timing; deliverables and reporting definitions; sponsored labeling and independence; no traffic/booking guarantee; source outages and pause/make-good rules; cancellation/refund terms; permitted logo/name use; privacy/data sharing; intellectual-property warranties appropriate to each party; liability/indemnity allocation; dispute law; and signature/acceptance evidence. Do not insert a blanket indemnity you cannot finance or broad rights you do not own.

# Part V — Solo-founder outreach operating system

## CRM structure and stages
Use one organization/account record, then add actual contacts after the recipient verifies their role. Avoid creating separate inflated accounts for Expedia, Hotels.com and Vrbo when pursuing the group program. Keep a notes field for corporate ownership uncertainty and separate resort-specific conversations where authority truly differs.

Stages: researched → source reverified → contacted → replied → qualified → pilot proposed → pilot active → paid active → renewed. Terminal states: not now, no fit, lost and opted out. A vendor quote is not “paid active revenue”; mark its role and expected cost. A permission approval is a valuable access outcome but not a sale.

Qualification requires a real need, correct authorized person, specific scope, realistic value/cost and an agreed next step. Only then add a numeric opportunity amount and close date. The supplied prospect deal ranges should not be summed as a weighted revenue pipeline. Use account-level suppression and record opt-out immediately.

## Response assumptions and stop rules
For small, genuinely relevant one-to-one messages, use a **10–25% reply range only as a planning assumption**, with lower rates for large corporate buyers and potentially higher rates for a clearly routed technical request. Responses include “no,” routing and questions—not just qualified opportunities. No measured PeakCam outreach baseline exists. Twenty contacts might yield two to five replies, and zero paid deals is entirely plausible.

Send no more than three total messages over about sixteen days unless the recipient engages. Stop on opt-out, clear no-fit, repeated routing failure or no reply after the final note. Revisit a “not this season” only at the date they explicitly suggest. Never contact every named employee at an organization simultaneously or use consumer support aggressively to bypass a refusal.

## Call agenda and follow-through
Confirm who owns the decision; ask how they currently solve the specific source/referral problem; show the exact proposed page and actual metrics; agree on permitted scope, price/cost and the success measure; identify any legal/provider approvals; agree a dated next step. Send a short written recap and attach only approved materials. Never say “we’re partners” before the agreement actually permits that statement.

## Weekly metrics
New verified accounts contacted; reply rate; correct-person routing; qualified conversations; written source approvals; proposals; pilots live; invoices paid; approved affiliate conversions; renewals; opted-out accounts; founder hours per qualified outcome. Track permissions and commercial revenue in separate columns. The goal is five useful small relationships, not a large list of unreturned messages.
